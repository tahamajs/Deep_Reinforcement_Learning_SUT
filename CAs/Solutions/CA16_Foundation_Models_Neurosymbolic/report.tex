\\begin{abstract}
This paper introduces the Hybrid Foundation-Neurosymbolic Continual Learner (HFNSCL), a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to address critical limitations of current deep RL agents: sample inefficiency, lack of interpretability, and catastrophic forgetting. We synthesize three advanced paradigms: Decision Transformers (DTs) for sample-efficient sequential modeling, neurosymbolic integration for interpretable reasoning, and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) for continual learning. The HFNSCL leverages a pre-trained Decision Transformer as a core policy, augmented by a symbolic knowledge base and inference engine for logical reasoning and constraint application. Continual learning is facilitated through EWC, which regularizes parameter updates to preserve knowledge from previously learned tasks. We provide detailed mathematical derivations for the combined loss functions and propose an architectural synthesis that allows for robust adaptation across diverse, evolving environments while maintaining transparency in decision-making. Preliminary experimental designs are outlined, demonstrating the potential for superior performance in complex, dynamic tasks.
\\end{abstract}

\\section{Introduction}
Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has achieved remarkable successes across various domains, from game playing to robotics. However, significant challenges persist, particularly concerning sample efficiency, the "black-box" nature of deep neural networks, and the inability of agents to learn continuously without forgetting previously acquired skills. These limitations hinder the deployment of DRL agents in real-world scenarios that demand rapid adaptation, verifiable decision-making, and lifelong learning capabilities.

Foundation Models, such as Decision Transformers \cite{chen2021decision}, have shown promise in addressing sample inefficiency by framing RL as a sequence modeling problem, allowing for leveraging large-scale offline datasets. Concurrently, neurosymbolic AI offers a pathway to more interpretable and robust agents by integrating the strengths of neural perception with symbolic reasoning \cite{garnelo2019neural}. Furthermore, continual learning techniques, like Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}, are crucial for agents operating in dynamic environments where new tasks emerge over time, preventing catastrophic forgetting.

This paper proposes a novel framework, the Hybrid Foundation-Neurosymbolic Continual Learner (HFNSCL), which synergistically combines these three paradigms. Our primary contribution is an architecture that enables an RL agent to be sample-efficient through a pre-trained Decision Transformer, interpretable via a neurosymbolic reasoning module, and continually adaptable through EWC regularization. We detail the mathematical underpinnings of this synthesis and outline a comprehensive implementation strategy designed for real-world application.

\\section{Related Work}
Our work builds upon recent advancements in several key areas of artificial intelligence.

\\subsection{Foundation Models in RL}
The concept of Foundation Models \cite{bommasani2022opportunities} has gained traction, referring to large models pre-trained on broad data that can be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks. In RL, Decision Transformers (DTs) \cite{chen2021decision} exemplify this by treating RL as a sequence prediction problem. DTs predict actions autoregressively based on desired returns, states, and past actions, eliminating the need for traditional value functions or policy gradients. Trajectory Transformers \cite{janner2021trajectory} further extend this by modeling entire episode trajectories. While powerful for offline RL and few-shot adaptation, these models often lack inherent interpretability and can struggle with catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on new tasks.

\\subsection{Neurosymbolic Reinforcement Learning}
Neurosymbolic AI seeks to combine the sub-symbolic processing capabilities of neural networks with the symbolic reasoning abilities of traditional AI \cite{garcez2019neurosymbolic}. In RL, this integration aims to imbue agents with human-like reasoning, common sense, and interpretability \cite{wang2021learning}. Approaches include using neural networks for perceptual grounding of symbols, employing symbolic logic for planning or rule extraction, and incorporating logic-based regularization into deep learning objectives. Existing neurosymbolic RL methods often focus on specific aspects like causal reasoning or explainable policies but rarely integrate with large-scale foundation models or robust continual learning.

\\subsection{Continual Learning}
Continual learning (also known as lifelong learning) is a paradigm where models learn sequentially from a stream of tasks, accumulating knowledge over time without forgetting previously learned information \cite{parisi2019continual}. Catastrophic forgetting is a major challenge in this field. Regularization-based methods, such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} and Synaptic Intelligence \cite{zenke2017continual}, mitigate forgetting by protecting important parameters. Rehearsal-based methods store and replay past experiences, while architectural methods (e.g., Progressive Neural Networks \cite{rusu2016progressive}) expand model capacity. Our work integrates EWC as a foundational continual learning mechanism, which can be further augmented by other strategies.

Our HFNSCL framework uniquely synthesizes these areas, addressing the joint challenges of sample efficiency, interpretability, and continual adaptation, which are often tackled in isolation in existing literature.

\\section{Methodology}
The Hybrid Foundation-Neurosymbolic Continual Learner (HFNSCL) integrates a Decision Transformer (DT) as its core policy with a Neurosymbolic Reasoning Module and an Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) mechanism for continual adaptation.

\\subsection{Overall Architecture}
The HFNSCL agent operates in a closed loop with the environment. It comprises:
\\begin{enumerate}
    \\item \\textbf{Foundation Policy (Decision Transformer)}: The primary component for sequential decision-making, pre-trained on diverse trajectories.
    \\item \\textbf{Neurosymbolic Reasoning Module}: Provides interpretable insights and constraints. This module consists of:
    \\begin{itemize}
        \\item \\textit{Perceptual Neural Network}: Extracts high-level features and infers symbolic predicates from raw observations.
        \\item \\textit{Symbolic Knowledge Base (SKB)}: Stores logical facts and rules relevant to the domain.
        \\item \\textit{Inference Engine}: Applies logical reasoning over the SKB to generate symbolic explanations or impose constraints.
    \\end{itemize}
    \\item \\textbf{Continual Learning Manager}: Manages knowledge retention across tasks, primarily through EWC.
\\end{enumerate}

The information flow is as follows: The environment state is fed to both the DT and the Perceptual Neural Network. The neural network infers symbolic predicates, which update the SKB. The Inference Engine then generates symbolic constraints or explanations that can modulate the action proposed by the DT. The DT's learning process is regularized by EWC to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

\\subsection{Decision Transformer (DT) with Auxiliary Losses}
The DT models RL as a sequence prediction task. Given a trajectory \\(\\tau = (\\mathbf{s}_0, \\mathbf{a}_0, r_0, \\dots, \\mathbf{s}_T, \\mathbf{a}_T, r_T)\\) and target returns \\(R_t\\), the DT aims to predict actions. The primary loss is a behavioral cloning loss on actions:
\\begin{equation}
L_{action} = \\frac{1}{T} \\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \\| \\hat{\\mathbf{a}}_t - \\mathbf{a}_t \\|_2^2
\\end{equation}
where \\(\\hat{\\mathbf{a}}_t\\) are the predicted actions and \\(\\mathbf{a}_t\\) are the ground truth actions from the expert trajectory. For discrete action spaces, cross-entropy loss is typically used. To enhance stability and predictive power, we incorporate auxiliary losses for state and return prediction:
\\begin{align}
L_{state} &= \\frac{1}{T} \\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \\| \\hat{\\mathbf{s}}_t - \\mathbf{s}_t \\|_2^2 \\\\
L_{return} &= \\frac{1}{T} \\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \\| \\hat{R}_t - R_t \\|_2^2
\\end{align}
The total Decision Transformer loss is a weighted sum:
\\begin{equation}
L_{DT\\_total} = L_{action} + \\beta_1 L_{state} + \\beta_2 L_{return}
\\end{equation}
where \\(\\beta_1, \\beta_2 \\ge 0\\) are hyperparameters balancing the auxiliary objectives.

\\subsection{Neurosymbolic Policy with Logic Regularization}
The neurosymbolic component augments the DT's policy with logical reasoning. Let the neural policy be \\(\\pi_{\\theta}(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})\\) and \\(g(\\mathbf{s})\\) be a function that extracts symbolic predicates from the state. We introduce a logic regularization term to ensure the agent's behavior aligns with explicit symbolic rules from the SKB.
For a set of symbolic rules \\(\\mathcal{R} = \\{\\text{rule}_k\\}_{k=1}^N\\), we can define a regularization loss that penalizes violations:
\\begin{equation}
L_{Logic} = \\gamma \\sum_{k=1}^N \\mathbb{I}(\\text{rule}_k \\text{ violated}) \\cdot C_k
\\end{equation}
where \\(\\mathbb{I}(\\cdot)\\) is the indicator function, which is 1 if rule \\(k\\) is violated and 0 otherwise, \\(C_k\\) is a positive cost associated with violating rule \\(k\\), and \\(\\gamma \\ge 0\\) is a hyperparameter for the strength of regularization. Rule violations are determined by the Inference Engine based on the current state and SKB. The combined neurosymbolic-DT loss becomes:
\\begin{equation}
L_{NeuroDT} = L_{DT\\_total} + L_{Logic}
\\end{equation}

\\subsection{Continual Learning with Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)}
To mitigate catastrophic forgetting across a sequence of tasks \\(\\mathcal{T} = \\{T_1, T_2, \\dots, T_M\\}\\), we employ Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. When learning a new task \\(T_k\\), the loss function includes a quadratic penalty that discourages significant deviations of parameters important for previous tasks. The EWC penalty for task \\(T_k\\) is:
\\begin{equation}
L_{EWC} = \\sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \\sum_i \\frac{\\lambda_j}{2} F_{j,i} (\\thet-i - \\theta_{j,i}^*)^2
\\end{equation}
Here, \\(\\thet-i\\) is the \\(i^{th}\\) parameter of the current model, \\(\\theta_{j,i}^*\\) is the optimal value of \\(i^{th}\\) parameter after learning task \\(T_j\\), \\(F_{j,i}\\) is the \\(i^{th}\\) diagonal element of the Fisher Information Matrix for task \\(T_j\\), and \\(\\lambda_j \\ge 0\\) is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of regularization for task \\(T_j\\).

The Fisher Information Matrix \\(F\\) for a task \\(T\\) is given by:
\\begin{equation}
F = \\mathbb{E}_{(\\mathbf{s}, \\mathbf{a}) \\sim \\pi_\\theta} \\left[ \\left( \\nabla_\\theta \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s}) \\right) \\left( \\nabla_\\theta \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s}) \\right)^T \\right]
\\end{equation}
In practice, a diagonal approximation is often used:
\\begin{equation}
F_i = \\mathbb{E}_{(\\mathbf{s}, \\mathbf{a}) \\sim \\pi_\\theta} \\left[ \\left( \\frac{\\partial \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})}{\\partial \\thet-i} \\right)^2 \\right]
\\end{equation}
The total loss for the HFNSCL agent when learning task \\(T_k\\) is the sum of the neurosymbolic-DT loss and the EWC penalty:
\\begin{equation}
L_{Total} = L_{NeuroDT} + L_{EWC}
\\end{equation}

\\section{Experiments}
We propose a series of experiments to evaluate the HFNSCL framework across key metrics: sample efficiency, interpretability, and continual learning performance.

\\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\\subsubsection{Environments}
We will utilize three main types of environments:
\\begin{enumerate}
    \\item \\textbf{MultiModalGridWorld}: A synthetic environment where agents receive both visual (pixel) observations and discrete symbolic predicates (e.g., \\textit{obstacle\_ahead}, \\textit{goal\_reached}). This environment is ideal for evaluating neurosymbolic integration.
    \\item \\textbf{Continual Task Sequence (CTS) Environments}: A custom suite of environments presenting a sequence of distinct tasks (e.g., varying reward functions, obstacle layouts, or goal locations). This will test catastrophic forgetting and knowledge transfer.
    \\item \\textbf{D4RL Benchmarks}: Standard offline RL datasets like \\textit{Maze2D-UMaze-v1} and \\textit{HalfCheetah-v2} will be used for pre-training the Decision Transformer component and evaluating its offline performance.
\\end{enumerate}

\\subsubsection{Baselines}
We will compare HFNSCL against the following baselines:
\\begin{enumerate}
    \\item \\textbf{Standard Decision Transformer (DT)}: To evaluate the benefits of neurosymbolic and continual learning components.
    \\item \\textbf{DRL Agent with Logic Regularization (without DT or EWC)}: To isolate the impact of neurosymbolic integration.
    \\item \\textbf{Continual Learning Baseline (e.g., EWC only)}: To assess the contribution of the foundation model and neurosymbolic parts to continual adaptation.
    \\item \\textbf{Human-AI Collaborative Agent (RLHF-only)}: For tasks involving human feedback, a baseline purely based on human preference learning.
\\end{enumerate}

\\subsubsection{Metrics}
\\begin{enumerate}
    \\item \\textbf{Sample Efficiency}: Evaluated by the number of environment interactions or offline data required to achieve a target performance level.
    \\item \\textbf{Interpretability}: Measured by the fidelity of symbolic explanations to agent behavior, consistency of inferred predicates, and human evaluation of policy transparency.
    \\item \\textbf{Continual Learning}:
    \\begin{itemize}
        \\item \\textit{Forward Transfer}: Performance on new tasks as a function of prior learning.
        \\item \\textit{Backward Transfer/Forgetting}: Retention of knowledge on old tasks after learning new ones.
        \\item \\textit{Average Performance}: Mean performance across all learned tasks.
    \\end{itemize}
    \\item \\textbf{Robustness}: Performance under noisy or adversarial inputs, and adherence to symbolic safety constraints.
    \\item \\textbf{Ethical Compliance}: Qualitative assessment of fairness and bias using metrics like demographic parity if applicable.
\\end{enumerate}

\\section{Ablation Study}
To understand the individual contributions of each component within the HFNSCL framework, we propose the following ablation studies:
\\begin{enumerate}
    \\item \\textbf{HFNSCL w/o Neurosymbolic Module}: This variant will remove the logic regularization and symbolic reasoning, relying solely on the DT and EWC. This will quantify the impact of interpretability and domain knowledge injection.
    \\item \\textbf{HFNSCL w/o EWC}: This variant will remove the EWC regularization, allowing for catastrophic forgetting. This will highlight the importance of continual learning for sequential task adaptation.
    \\item \\textbf{HFNSCL w/o Pre-trained DT}: This variant will initialize the Decision Transformer from scratch for each task, instead of leveraging pre-training. This will demonstrate the sample efficiency benefits of the foundation model component.
    \\item \\textbf{Varying Logic Regularization Strength}: Investigate the effect of the hyperparameter \\(\\gamma\\) on interpretability and task performance.
    \\item \\textbf{Different EWC \\(\\lambda\\) values}: Study the trade-off between plasticity and stability with varying \\(\\lambda\\) values.
\\end{enumerate}

\\section{Conclusion and Broader Impact}
This paper presented the Hybrid Foundation-Neurosymbolic Continual Learner (HFNSCL), a novel approach for developing robust, interpretable, and adaptable reinforcement learning agents. By synthesizing Decision Transformers, neurosymbolic AI, and continual learning techniques, we aim to overcome critical limitations of current DRL systems. The proposed framework holds significant promise for applications in autonomous systems, healthcare, and human-robot interaction, where trust, safety, and lifelong learning are paramount.

The broader impact of HFNSCL extends to fostering more transparent and ethically responsible AI. By providing interpretable decision-making, it can aid in auditing and ensuring fairness. The continual learning aspect supports agents operating sustainably over long periods, reducing the need for retraining. However, careful consideration must be given to potential biases in pre-training data for foundation models and the complexity of managing large symbolic knowledge bases. Future work will involve extensive empirical validation and real-world deployment considerations.

\\section{Detailed Proofs}
\\subsection{Fisher Information Matrix Derivation}
The Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) quantifies the amount of information that an observable random variable carries about an unknown parameter of a distribution that models the random variable. For a policy \\(\\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})\\) parameterized by \\(\\theta\\), the FIM for a task \\(T\\) is defined as:
\\begin{align*}
F_{ij} &= \\mathbb{E}_{(\\mathbf{s}, \\mathbf{a}) \\sim \\pi_\\theta} \\left[ \\left( \\frac{\\partial \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})}{\\partial \\thet-i} \\right) \\left( \\frac{\\partial \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})}{\\partial \\theta_j} \\right) \\right] \\\\
&= -\\mathbb{E}_{(\\mathbf{s}, \\mathbf{a}) \\sim \\pi_\\theta} \\left[ \\frac{\\partial^2 \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})}{\\partial \\thet-i \\partial \\theta_j} \\right]
\\end{align*}
For the diagonal approximation used in EWC, we only consider the diagonal elements:
\\begin{equation*}
F_i = F_{ii} = \\mathbb{E}_{(\\mathbf{s}, \\mathbf{a}) \\sim \\pi_\\theta} \\left[ \\left( \\frac{\\partial \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})}{\\partial \\thet-i} \\right)^2 \\right]
\\end{equation*}
In practice, this expectation is approximated by Monte Carlo sampling from the agent's experience (e.g., transitions collected from the replay buffer or on-policy rollouts). For a set of \\(N\\) samples \\(\\{(\\mathbf{s}_m, \\mathbf{a}_m)\\}_{m=1}^N\\):
\\begin{equation*}
F_i \\approx \\frac{1}{N} \\sum_{m=1}^N \\left( \\frac{\\partial \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}_m|\\mathbf{s}_m)}{\\partial \\thet-i} \\right)^2
\\end{equation*}
The gradient \\(\\nabla_\\theta \\log \\pi_\\theta(\\mathbf{a}|\\mathbf{s})\\) is computed via backpropagation through the policy network.
